Meconium ileus (MI) and meconium plug syndrome (MPS) are both conditions related to meconium obstruction in the intestines, but they differ in their underlying mechanisms and clinical implications:

1. **Underlying Cause**:
   - **Meconium Ileus**: This condition is commonly associated with cystic fibrosis (CF) and occurs due to the thickening of meconium, which leads to blockage of the ileum (the last part of the small intestine). The thickened meconium often results from abnormal secretions related to CF.
   - **Meconium Plug Syndrome**: MPS is characterized by a blockage typically caused by a meconium plug, which can lead to intestinal obstruction. It is often benign and not necessarily associated with cystic fibrosis, although it can be associated with other conditions.

2. **Demographics**:
   - **Meconium Ileus**: Primarily seen in neonates, particularly those with cystic fibrosis.
   - **Meconium Plug Syndrome**: It can also occur in newborns but is described as a common cause of low intestinal obstruction in infants more generally, irrespective of cystic fibrosis.

3. **Clinical Presentation**:
   - **Meconium Ileus**: Usually presents shortly after birth with signs of intestinal obstruction, such as abdominal distension and failure to pass meconium.
   - **Meconium Plug Syndrome**: May present with similar obstruction symptoms, but it is often noted that MPS can resolve without severe complications and does not inherently indicate an underlying malabsorption or pancreatic dysfunction as in cystic fibrosis.

4. **Management and Prognosis**:
   - **Meconium Ileus**: Treatment often requires interventions like enemas or surgical procedures if severe, given its association with cystic fibrosis.
   - **Meconium Plug Syndrome**: This condition typically resolves with less invasive measures, such as enemas, and often has a benign prognosis.

In summary, while both conditions involve intestinal obstruction due to meconium, they differ in their associations with cystic fibrosis, the nature of the obstruction, and their clinical courses.
